Real networks are not Poisson

If real networks were wired at random, how large could their best-connected node be? The Poisson tail answers precisely, and real networks miss it by hundreds of orders of magnitude.

  • Tests the Poisson prediction on data.
  • Nodes far above it → chance wiring is rejected; some mechanism concentrates links.

How large is the largest node? (Advanced Topic 3.B)

$$N\,P(K > k_{\max}) \approx 1, \qquad N\,P(K < k_{\min}) \approx 1.$$
$K$
degree of a randomly chosen node (a random variable)
$P(K > k)$
fraction of nodes with more than $k$ links, $\sum_{k' > k} p_{k'}$
$N\,P(K > k_{\max})$
expected number of such nodes; = 1 means "about one node"
$k_{\min}$
smallest degree, from the lower tail

$\langle k\rangle = 10$: $k_{\max} \approx 21$ ($N = 10^3$), 28 ($10^6$), 34 ($10^9$). Power law, $\gamma = 2.5$: $k_{\max} \sim N^{2/3} = 10^6$ at $N = 10^9$.

A random society

A person knows about $\langle k\rangle \approx 1{,}000$ people. The figure plots $N p_k$, the expected number of people with $k$ acquaintances.

$N = 7\times10^9$: most connected person ≈ 1,200 acquaintances, least ≈ 800. $\sigma_k \approx 32$: two thirds know 968–1,032 people, 99.7% know 905–1,095. No celebrities, no hermits.

The book's networks against Poisson

Each network vs the Poisson with its own $\langle k\rangle$. Log bins (widening with $k$) and the CCDF $P(K \ge k)$ show the tail; linear bins reproduce the notebook (see How (not) to plot $p_k$).

Collaboration ($\langle k\rangle = 8.08$): Poisson $k_{\max} \approx 21$. Nodes with $k \ge 50$: 244 observed, $6.5\times10^{-19}$ expected. Expected nodes with $k = 279$: $10^{-309}$.

Every network exceeds its Poisson $k_{\max}$. Smallest gap: power grid (19 vs 10, an engineered network); largest: WWW (10,721 vs 21).

  • Builds on the Poisson tail of Degree distribution.
  • Contrast: power-law natural cutoff $k_{\max} \sim k_{\min} N^{1/(\gamma-1)}$ grows like a power of $N$ (Ch 4).
  • Explained by growth + preferential attachment (Barabási–Albert, Ch 5).
  • Better null model once hubs are accepted: the configuration model keeps every degree.

Random wiring predicts no node above about 20 collaborators; the data have dozens above 100. Which processes could produce these hubs? (Notebook II.3.)

Preferential attachment: visible scientists attract collaborators (Ch 5). Fitness: some nodes are intrinsically more attractive (Ch 6). Longevity: early nodes had more time. Team size: a paper with hundreds of authors gives each hundreds of links at once, an artefact of the bipartite projection.

Random society, $\langle k\rangle = 1{,}000$: $k_{\min} \approx 807$. What does this mean?

  1. Most people know 807 others
  2. Nobody has more than 807 acquaintances
  3. Nobody would be a near-hermit: even the least connected knows ~800 people
  4. The data show 807 hermits
$N\,P(K < k_{\min}) \approx 1$: about one person falls below 807.

Power grid: observed $k_{\max} = 19$, Poisson 10, the smallest gap of the eight. Why?

  2. Physical cost limits how many lines a substation can carry
  3. It is the smallest network
  4. Its ⟨k⟩ is highest
