Real networks are sparse

Of all the links that could exist, real networks contain a tiny fraction, the density, and it shrinks as they grow. Density decides how networks can be stored and computed with, and sets the random model to compare against.

Possible links and density

$$L_{\max} = \binom{N}{2} = \frac{N(N-1)}{2}, \qquad \text{density} = \frac{L}{L_{\max}} = \frac{\langle k\rangle}{N-1}.$$
$\binom{N}{2}$
"$N$ choose 2": number of node pairs
$L_{\max}$
links of the complete graph (all pairs linked)
$L/L_{\max}$
density, between 0 and 1
$N-1$
largest possible degree

The second form uses $L = N\langle k\rangle/2$. Dense: $L$ close to $L_{\max}$. Sparse: $L \ll L_{\max}$ ("much smaller"). All the book's networks are sparse: $\langle k\rangle$ between 2.7 and 9.1 while $N$ spans more than two orders of magnitude.

Collaboration: $L_{\max} = 23{,}133 \times 23{,}132/2 \approx 2.68\times10^8$; $L = 93{,}439$; density $= 8.08/23{,}132 \approx 3.5\times10^{-4}$.

The eight reference networks

On log-log axes each tick multiplies by 10, and $L = c\,N^a$ is a straight line of slope $a$: slope 2 for $L_{\max}$, slope 1 for fixed $\langle k\rangle$.

The networks lie 4–5 orders of magnitude below $L_{\max}$, along the grey lines of constant $\langle k\rangle$: $L$ grows like $N$, not $N^2$. (They are eight different systems, not one growing network.)

What sparsity looks like

The collaboration matrix has $23{,}133^2 \approx 5.35\times 10^8$ entries; only $2L = 186{,}878$ (0.035%) are non-zero.

Storing a network

A dense matrix needs $N^2$ entries. Memory proportional to $L$ is enough for an edge list (one pair of node ids per link) or a sparse matrix (CSR: all neighbour lists, $2L$ ids, plus $N+1$ offsets).

  • Sparse storage makes million-node networks fit in memory.
  • Algorithms that touch each link a few times (BFS, components, triangles) stay fast.
  • The fair random comparison links each pair with probability $p$ = density.

Why do we store networks as edge lists or sparse matrices? How much memory would a dense matrix need for the WWW sample ($N \approx 3\times10^5$), or for Facebook ($N \approx 3\times10^9$)? (Notebook I.3.)

WWW: $N^2 \approx 1.1\times10^{11}$ entries, 13 GB at one bit each, 106 GB at one byte; its 1.1 million links fit in a 9 MB edge list. $N = 3\times10^9$: $9\times10^{18}$ bits, about an exabyte, versus a few terabytes for an edge list with $\langle k\rangle \approx 200$. Try the presets above.
  • Builds on $\langle k\rangle = 2L/N$ (Degree) and the adjacency matrix, here almost all zeros.
  • Contrast: sparse yet close, distances stay short (Paths) and most nodes form one giant piece (Connectedness).
  • Sets the null model: G(N, p) with $p$ = density; its giant component depends on $\langle k\rangle$, not $N$ (Evolution).
