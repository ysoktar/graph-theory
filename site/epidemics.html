Spreading & vaccination

Viruses, rumours and computer worms spread along links. On a scale-free network the hubs that hold it together also carry outbreaks everywhere, which changes how epidemics behave and how to stop them (Chapter 10).

The epidemic threshold

In the SIR model a node is susceptible, infected or recovered. Here each infected node passes the infection to each susceptible neighbour with probability $T$ (the transmissibility) and then recovers. An outbreak can reach a finite share of the network only if an infected node, reached through a link, infects on average more than one further node. That gives the threshold

$$T_c = \frac{\langle k\rangle}{\langle k^2\rangle - \langle k\rangle}.$$

For a random network $\langle k^2\rangle - \langle k\rangle = \langle k\rangle^2$ and $T_c = 1/\langle k\rangle$. For a scale-free network with $\gamma < 3$, $\langle k^2\rangle$ diverges with $N$ and $T_c \to 0$: even a barely contagious disease can spread (Pastor-Satorras and Vespignani 2001).

The same $\langle k^2\rangle$ that makes heavy-tailed networks robust to random failures (Ch 8) makes them vulnerable to epidemics, and the remedy mirrors the attack: remove the hubs, here by vaccinating them.

Back to the notebook's closing question: if you could vaccinate 1% of a population whose contact network is scale-free, whom would you choose, and why does $\langle k^2\rangle$ matter?

The hubs, if you know who they are: removing them cuts $\langle k^2\rangle$ much more than $\langle k\rangle$ and raises $T_c$. If you do not know the network, acquaintance vaccination finds hubs with high probability, because a random contact of a random person has on average $\langle k^2\rangle/\langle k\rangle$ contacts, more than the average person's $\langle k\rangle$.

A contact network has $\langle k\rangle = 4$ and $\langle k^2\rangle = 84$. What is the epidemic threshold $T_c$?

  1. 0.25
  2. 0.05
  3. 0.5
  4. 0.012
$T_c = \langle k\rangle/(\langle k^2\rangle - \langle k\rangle) = 4/80 = 0.05$. A random network with the same $\langle k\rangle$ would have $T_c = 1/4 = 0.25$, five times higher.
