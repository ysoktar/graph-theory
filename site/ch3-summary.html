Real networks are not random

Which features of real networks need an explanation beyond chance? Hold the random network against the data, property by property; the failures decide where Chapter 4 goes next.

  • Erdős and Rényi never claimed real networks are random: the model is a baseline.
  • Reproduced → no special explanation needed. Missed → a non-random process is at work.

The model fails on the two most local features: how many links a node has (hubs) and whether neighbours are linked (triangles). Chapter 4 replaces Poisson with a power law; clustering returns in Chapter 9.

  • Summarises degrees, components, distances, clustering: each a Part I quantity, predicted then tested.
  • Motivates scale-free networks (Ch 4) and the Barabási–Albert model (Ch 5), which explains the hubs.
  • Null model hierarchy: $G(N, L)$ keeps $N, L$; the configuration model also keeps every degree.

The random model gets distances roughly right for six of the eight networks. Does that make it a good model of their wiring?

No. Almost any network with a few long-range links is a small world (Watts–Strogatz), so agreement there is weak evidence. The discriminating properties, degrees and clustering, are the ones it gets wrong.
