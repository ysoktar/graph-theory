Generating networks with a given degree distribution

To ask which properties of a real network follow from its degrees alone, we need random networks that share those degrees and nothing else. Section 4.8 gives two ways to build them.

The configuration model

Give node $i$ exactly $k_i$ stubs (half-links), then pair all stubs uniformly at random. Every network with the given degree sequence is then equally likely, but the procedure can create self-loops and repeated links. The probability that nodes $i$ and $j$ are linked is approximately

$$p_{ij} \approx \frac{k_i k_j}{2L}.$$

Degree-preserving randomization

Start from the real network and repeatedly swap the ends of two random links. Every swap keeps all degrees exactly, and after enough swaps all other structure is scrambled. Unlike the configuration model, no link is ever lost.

What the degree sequence explains

  • By construction, the degree sequence reproduces $k_{\max}$ and $\langle k^2\rangle$, which $G(N, L)$ gets badly wrong.
  • The randomized networks are usually smaller than both the real network and $G(N, L)$: hubs act as shortcuts, while in the real network many links are spent inside tightly knit groups.
  • For the collaboration network $\langle C\rangle$ collapses by more than two orders of magnitude and the positive assortativity disappears. Triangles and degree correlations come from other mechanisms: teams of co-authors, communities (Ch 9), homophily (Ch 7).

Comparing a network with its degree-preserving randomization is one of the most useful habits in network science: whatever survives randomization is explained by the degrees; whatever disappears needs another explanation.

After degree-preserving randomization, the assortativity of the collaboration network drops from 0.13 to about 0. What does that show?

  1. The degree sequence was changed by the swaps
  2. Assortativity cannot be measured in random networks
  3. The positive correlation between the degrees of linked scientists is not a consequence of the degree sequence
  4. The real network is not connected
The swaps keep every degree, so anything they destroy was not implied by the degrees. Hubs collaborating with hubs is a property of how the links are arranged.
