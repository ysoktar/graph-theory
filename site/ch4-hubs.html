The largest hub

How many links does the best-connected node have? In a random network the answer barely changes as the network grows. In a scale-free network it grows like a power of the network's size (Section 4.3).

The natural cutoff

The largest degree $k_{\max}$ of a network of $N$ nodes is roughly the degree above which we expect to find a single node:

$$N \int_{k_{\max}}^{\infty} p(k)\, dk \approx 1 .$$

For the continuous power law $p(k) = (\gamma-1)\,k_{\min}^{\gamma-1}k^{-\gamma}$ the integral is $(k_{\max}/k_{\min})^{-(\gamma-1)}$, which gives the natural cutoff

$$k_{\max} \approx k_{\min}\, N^{1/(\gamma-1)} .$$

For an exponential distribution $p(k) = \lambda e^{-\lambda(k - k_{\min})}$ the same calculation gives $k_{\max} \approx k_{\min} + \ln N/\lambda$: logarithmic growth. A Poisson tail falls faster than an exponential one, so its maximum grows even more slowly, roughly like $\ln N/\ln\ln N$.

Simulation

At $N = 10^6$ the power law ($\gamma = 2.5$) produces a hub with about 10,000 links, the exponential distribution with the same average one with about 29, and the Poisson distribution one with about 14. Hubs are a direct consequence of the heavy tail, not of anything else in the network.

The book's networks

For a real network, the natural cutoff of its fitted tail is $k_{\min}\,n_{\text{tail}}^{1/(\gamma-1)}$, where $n_{\text{tail}}$ is the number of nodes with $k \ge k_{\min}$ (notebook cell 59, here for all eight networks).

  • Every observed hub is far beyond what a random network allows: 7 to 510 times the Poisson value, except for the power grid (19 against 10).
  • Where the fitted exponent is close to 2 (email, WWW), the natural cutoff overshoots by a factor of 8 to 10: real tails bend down at the highest degrees, because a node cannot maintain unlimited links and the network is finite.
  • The notebook's three examples all overshoot, but that is not general: the metabolic hub (highlighted) is three times larger than its cutoff. Its fitted tail starts at $k_{\min} = 5$, and its few largest hubs lie above the fitted line.
