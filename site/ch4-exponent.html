Real networks

To tell a power law with $\gamma > 3$ from a random network's degree distribution you need a tail long enough to see the straight line. How large must $N$ be?

The tail must span at least two orders of magnitude, so $k_{\max}/k_{\min} = N^{1/(\gamma-1)} \gtrsim 100$, that is $N \gtrsim 100^{\gamma-1}$. For $\gamma = 3.5$ that is $N \gtrsim 10^5$; for $\gamma = 5$ already $N \gtrsim 10^8$. The book makes this argument in Section 4.7: for large $\gamma$ the scale-free property is practically invisible in networks of realistic size.
