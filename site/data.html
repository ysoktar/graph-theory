The data: eight real networks

The book tests every idea on ten "reference networks" (Table 2.1). The notebook and this site use eight of them, from biology, technology, society and information; the actor and citation networks are left out because they are too large for a classroom. Measuring the same quantities across such different systems is how we learn which properties are general.

Explore a network

Compare the power grid with the WWW or email. The grid is sparse and almost lattice-like: no node has more than 19 links, and its degree distribution is close to the Poisson curve. The WWW and email networks are dominated by a few hubs, and in the tail they lie orders of magnitude above the Poisson curve. Chapters 3 and 4 explain the difference.

All eight at a glance

$N$, $L$
the numbers of nodes and links (as undirected, simple networks)
$\langle k\rangle$, $k_{\max}$
the average and the largest degree (Degree)
$\sigma_k/\langle k\rangle$
the spread of the degrees relative to their mean; about $1/\sqrt{\langle k\rangle}$ for a Poisson distribution
giant comp.
the share of nodes in the largest component (Connectedness)
$\langle C\rangle$
the average clustering coefficient (Clustering)
$\langle d\rangle$
the mean distance in the giant component, from BFS runs from 300 random sources (Paths)
$r$
the degree correlation coefficient (assortativity) (Degree correlations)
$\hat\gamma$, $k_{\min}$
the exponent and the start of a power-law fit to the degrees $k \ge k_{\min}$, by the method of Clauset, Shalizi and Newman (Estimating the exponent)
$p$
the p-value of a bootstrap goodness-of-fit test with 1,000 replicates; a power law is plausible if $p \ge 0.1$

How the data were prepared

  1. The edge lists come from the book's website (networks.zip, 141 MB).
  2. Each network is made undirected and simple, as in the notebook: directions are ignored, self-loops and repeated links are removed, and the nodes are relabelled 0…N−1.
  3. tools/preprocess.py computes the degree histograms, components, clustering, distances, assortativity, power-law fits and null-model counterparts and stores them as small JSON files. The six smaller networks also ship as full edge lists (about 1 MB), so pages can analyse them live; the Internet and WWW (0.6 and 1.1 million links) ship as summaries only.

Five of the eight networks are directed in reality. What information is lost when we treat an email network as undirected? Which questions could no longer be answered?

Direction tells who initiates contact: mailing lists send far more than they receive, popular addresses receive far more than they send. One undirected degree merges both, and reciprocated pairs collapse into one link. Questions about information flow, reachability ("can a message get from A to B?") or the book's separate in- and out-degree exponents need the directed network.
  • Real data are the test every model must pass.
  • Eight domains with the same measurements show which properties are shared (sparsity, short distances, hubs) and which are not (the grid).
  • Part I defines each column of the table.
  • Part II compares every column with a random network of the same $N$ and $L$ (Real networks are not random).
  • Part III fits the degree tails with power laws (Estimating the exponent).

In which network is the largest hub linked to more than 10% of all nodes?

  1. Email ($k_{\max} = 6{,}553$, $N = 57{,}194$)
  2. WWW ($k_{\max} = 10{,}721$, $N = 325{,}729$)
  3. Collaboration ($k_{\max} = 279$, $N = 23{,}133$)
  4. Power grid ($k_{\max} = 19$, $N = 4{,}941$)
$6{,}553/57{,}194 \approx 11\%$: one address is in contact with one node in nine.

In the email network, both the link $u \to v$ and the link $v \to u$ exist. What do they become after the network is made undirected and simple?

  1. One link.
  2. Two links.
  3. Nothing: both are removed.
  4. A link of weight 2.
Reciprocated pairs merge into one link, which is why $L$ is smaller than the book's directed counts.

The protein edge list has 2,930 lines: 225 of them are self-loops and none is repeated. How many links $L$ does the simple network have?

  1. 2,930
  2. 2,705
  3. 3,155
  4. 2,018
$2{,}930 - 225 = 2{,}705$ links; $\langle k\rangle = 2 \times 2{,}705/2{,}018 \approx 2.68$.

The ratio $k_{\max}/\langle k\rangle$ of the largest to the average degree is about 7 for the power grid (19 / 2.67). What is it for the WWW ($k_{\max} = 10{,}721$, $\langle k\rangle = 6.69$)?

  1. 16
  2. 160
  3. 16,000
  4. About 1,600
$10{,}721/6.69 \approx 1{,}600$: the WWW's largest hub has as many links as 1,600 average pages, while the grid's has as many as 7 average nodes.
