Clustering

Your friends tend to know each other. The clustering coefficient measures how much, and it is the property that random networks most clearly fail to reproduce.

Node $i$'s $k_i$ neighbours can have at most $k_i(k_i-1)/2$ links among themselves. If $L_i$ exist, the local clustering coefficient is the fraction present:

$$C_i = \frac{2L_i}{k_i(k_i-1)}.$$
$k_i$
degree of node $i$
$L_i$
links among $i$'s neighbours = triangles through $i$
$k_i(k_i-1)/2$
possible links among the neighbours
$C_i$
probability that two neighbours of $i$ are linked; 0 to 1

For $k_i \le 1$ the formula is undefined; the book, the notebook and NetworkX set $C_i = 0$. Two network-wide summaries:

$$\langle C\rangle = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} C_i, \qquad C_\Delta = \frac{3\times \#\text{triangles}}{\#\text{connected triples}}.$$
$\langle C\rangle$
average clustering: every node weighs the same
connected triple
a node with two of its neighbours; node $i$ has $k_i(k_i-1)/2$
$C_\Delta$
global clustering (nx.transitivity, book Advanced Topic 2.A): share of triples closed into triangles
$3\times$
each triangle closes three triples, one per corner

Karate node 1: $k = 9$, $L_1 = 12$, so $C_1 = 24/72 \approx 0.33$.

Whole club: 45 triangles, $\sum_i k_i(k_i-1)/2 = 528$ triples, $C_\Delta = 135/528 \approx 0.256$; $\langle C\rangle = 0.571$.

Clustering in the karate club

The leaders have low clustering ($C_0 = 0.150$, $C_{33} = 0.110$), while many low-degree members sit in closed triangles: a hub's neighbours come from circles that do not know each other. This decreasing $C(k)$ returns in Part II.

Average or global clustering?

$\langle C\rangle$ weights nodes equally; $C_\Delta$ weights each node by its $k_i(k_i-1)/2$ triples, so hubs dominate it. The windmill graph ($t$ triangles sharing one node) shows the extreme.

$t = 10$: 20 leaves with $C_i = 1$, centre $C = 1/19$; $\langle C\rangle \approx 0.955$, $C_\Delta = 3/21 \approx 0.143$.

The book's eight networks

Why is $\langle C\rangle$ larger than $C_\Delta$ in the collaboration network ($0.633$ against $0.264$)? (Notebook I.7.)

Most scientists have few co-authors, often from a single paper, which closes all their triangles ($C_i \approx 1$); $\langle C\rangle$ counts them equally. $C_\Delta$ counts triples, $k(k-1)/2$ per scientist, so the few prolific authors, whose co-authors mostly do not know each other, dominate it. The windmill graph is the extreme case.
  • Clustering measures local cohesion: friends of friends become friends.
  • It is the clearest test of a model: a random network has $C \approx \langle k\rangle/N$, near 0 for large $N$.
  • $C(k)$ against $k$ reveals hierarchy: hubs bridge groups.
  • Counts triangles, $\tfrac16\mathrm{Tr}\,A^3$ (Adjacency matrix).
  • Partly made by projection: each paper becomes a clique (Bipartite networks).
  • Random-network prediction $C = p$ fails by orders of magnitude (Clustering in random networks).
  • Motivates the Watts–Strogatz model: high clustering with short distances.

Possible neighbour links grow like $k^2$; a hub joining separate circles closes only a small share of them.
