Formula sheet & glossary

The results of Chapters 2–10 as used on this site, each linked to the page that explores it.

Chapter 2 · Graph theory

Average degree
$$\langle k\rangle = \frac{1}{N}\sum_i k_i = \frac{2L}{N}$$

$N$ nodes, $L$ links, $k_i$ degree of node $i$.

Explore →
Handshaking lemma
$$\sum_i k_i = 2L \;\Rightarrow\; \text{the number of odd-degree nodes is even}$$

Each link adds 1 to two degrees.

Explore →
Degree distribution
$$p_k = N_k/N, \qquad \sum_k p_k = 1, \qquad \langle k^n\rangle = \sum_k k^n p_k$$

$N_k$ nodes with degree $k$; $\langle k^n\rangle$ $n$-th moment.

Explore →
Maximum number of links
$$L_{\max} = \binom{N}{2} = \frac{N(N-1)}{2}$$

Every pair of the $N$ nodes linked once.

Explore →
Adjacency matrix
$$A_{ij} = 1 \text{ if } i\text{–}j \text{ linked}; \quad k_i = \sum_j A_{ij}; \quad (A^n)_{ij} = \#\text{walks of length } n$$

$A_{ij}$ link indicator; $A^n$ counts walks of $n$ steps.

Explore →
Triangles
$$\#\triangle = \tfrac16 \operatorname{Tr} A^3$$

$\operatorname{Tr} A^3$ closed 3-walks; each triangle counted 6 times.

Explore →
Local clustering
$$C_i = \frac{2L_i}{k_i(k_i-1)}, \qquad \langle C\rangle = \frac1N \sum_i C_i$$

$L_i$ links among the $k_i$ neighbours of $i$.

Explore →
Global clustering (transitivity)
$$C_\Delta = \frac{3 \times \#\text{triangles}}{\#\text{connected triples}}$$

Triple: a node with two neighbours; ×3: each triangle closes three.

Explore →
Average distance
$$\langle d\rangle = \frac{1}{N(N-1)} \sum_{i \ne j} d_{ij} \quad\text{(connected pairs)}$$

$d_{ij}$ shortest-path length from $i$ to $j$.

Chapter 3 · Random networks

Number of links in G(N, p)
$$P(L) = \binom{L_{\max}}{L} p^L (1-p)^{L_{\max}-L}, \quad \langle L\rangle = p\frac{N(N-1)}{2}$$

$p$ link probability per pair; $L_{\max} = N(N-1)/2$.

Explore →
Average degree
$$\langle k\rangle = p(N-1)$$

$p$ link probability; $N-1$ possible partners per node.

Explore →
Binomial and Poisson degrees
$$p_k = \binom{N-1}{k} p^k (1-p)^{N-1-k} \;\approx\; e^{-\langle k\rangle}\frac{\langle k\rangle^k}{k!}$$

Poisson limit for large $N$, fixed $\langle k\rangle$.

Explore →
Width of the Poisson distribution
$$\sigma_k = \sqrt{\langle k\rangle}, \qquad \langle k^2\rangle = \langle k\rangle(1 + \langle k\rangle)$$

$\sigma_k$ standard deviation of the degrees.

Explore →
Largest and smallest degree
$$N\,P(K > k_{\max}) \approx 1, \qquad N\,P(K < k_{\min}) \approx 1$$

$k_{\max}$: about one node expected beyond it.

Explore →
Giant component
$$S = 1 - e^{-\langle k\rangle S}; \quad S > 0 \iff \langle k\rangle > 1$$

$S$ fraction of nodes in the giant component.

Explore →
Regimes
$$\begin{aligned} \langle k\rangle < 1 &:\ N_G \sim \ln N \\ \langle k\rangle = 1 &:\ N_G \sim N^{2/3} \\ \langle k\rangle > \ln N &:\ \text{connected} \end{aligned}$$

$N_G$ size of the largest component.

Explore →
Small world (with the Fronczak et al. 2004 correction)
$$\langle d\rangle \approx \frac{\ln N}{\ln\langle k\rangle} \approx \frac{\ln N - \gamma_E}{\ln\langle k\rangle} + \frac12$$

$\gamma_E \approx 0.577$ Euler's constant; second form: Fronczak et al. (2004).

Explore →
Clustering
$$\langle C\rangle = p = \frac{\langle k\rangle}{N-1}$$

Neighbours are linked with the same probability $p$ as any pair.

Chapter 4 · Scale-free networks

Discrete power law
$$p_k = \frac{k^{-\gamma}}{\zeta(\gamma, k_{\min})}, \quad k \ge k_{\min}$$

$\gamma$ exponent; $\zeta(\gamma, k_{\min})$ Hurwitz zeta, normalisation.

Explore →
Continuous power law
$$p(k) = (\gamma - 1) k_{\min}^{\gamma-1} k^{-\gamma}, \qquad P(K \ge k) = (k/k_{\min})^{-(\gamma-1)}$$

$P(K \ge k)$ CCDF: slope $-(\gamma - 1)$ on log-log axes.

Explore →
Natural cutoff
$$k_{\max} \approx k_{\min} N^{1/(\gamma-1)}; \quad \text{exponential: } k_{\min} + \ln N/\lambda$$

$k_{\max}$ expected largest degree; $\lambda$ exponential decay rate.

Explore →
Moments
$$\langle k^n\rangle = \frac{\gamma-1}{n-\gamma+1} k_{\min}^{\gamma-1}\big(k_{\max}^{n-\gamma+1} - k_{\min}^{n-\gamma+1}\big)$$

$n$ moment order; diverges with $k_{\max}$ when $n > \gamma - 1$.

Explore →
Distances
$$\langle d\rangle \sim \begin{cases} \text{const} & \gamma = 2 \\ \ln\ln N & 2<\gamma<3 \\ \ln N/\ln\ln N & \gamma = 3 \\ \ln N & \gamma > 3 \end{cases}$$

$\gamma$ degree exponent; smaller $\gamma$, shorter paths.

Explore →
Log-likelihood
$$\ln\mathcal L(\gamma) = -n \ln\zeta(\gamma, k_{\min}) - \gamma \sum_i \ln k_i$$

$n$ tail size ($k_i \ge k_{\min}$); maximise over $\gamma$.

Explore →
Closed-form MLE (k_min ≳ 6)
$$\hat\gamma \approx 1 + n\Big[\sum_i \ln\frac{k_i}{k_{\min} - 1/2}\Big]^{-1}, \qquad \sigma \approx \frac{\hat\gamma - 1}{\sqrt n}$$

$\hat\gamma$ estimate; $\sigma$ its standard error.

Explore →
KS distance
$$D = \max_{k \ge k_{\min}} |S(k) - P(k)|$$

$S$ data CDF, $P$ fitted CDF, both for $k \ge k_{\min}$.

Explore →
Configuration model
$$p_{ij} \approx \frac{k_i k_j}{2L}$$

$p_{ij}$ expected links between $i$ and $j$; $2L$ link ends.

Explore →

Chapters 5–10

BA preferential attachment
$$\Pi(k_i) = \frac{k_i}{\sum_j k_j}, \qquad k_i(t) = m (t/t_i)^{1/2}$$

$m$ links per new node; $t_i$ arrival time of $i$.

Explore →
BA degree distribution
$$p_k = \frac{2m(m+1)}{k(k+1)(k+2)} \sim 2m^2 k^{-3}$$

$\gamma = 3$ for every $m$; $k \ge m$.

Explore →
Fitness model
$$\Pi_i = \frac{\eta_i k_i}{\sum_j \eta_j k_j}, \qquad k_i(t) = m (t/t_i)^{\eta_i/C}$$

$\eta_i$ fitness; $C$ from $\int \rho(\eta)\,\eta/(C-\eta)\,d\eta = 1$ (1.255 for uniform).

Explore →
Degree correlation function
$$k_{nn}(k) = \sum_{k'} k' P(k' \mid k); \quad \text{neutral: } \frac{\langle k^2\rangle}{\langle k\rangle}$$

$k_{nn}(k)$ mean neighbour degree of degree-$k$ nodes.

Explore →
Structural cutoff
$$k_s \approx \sqrt{\langle k\rangle N}$$

Above $k_s$, random wiring would need multiple links between hubs.

Explore →
Molloy–Reed criterion
$$\kappa = \frac{\langle k^2\rangle}{\langle k\rangle} > 2$$

$\kappa$ mean degree of a node reached along a link.

Explore →
Random-failure threshold
$$f_c = 1 - \frac{1}{\kappa - 1}; \quad \text{random network: } 1 - \frac{1}{\langle k\rangle}$$

$f_c$ fraction of random failures that destroys the giant component.

Explore →
Modularity
$$Q = \sum_c \Big[\frac{L_c}{L} - \Big(\frac{k_c}{2L}\Big)^2\Big]$$

$L_c$ links inside $c$; $k_c$ total degree of $c$; $L$ all links.

Explore →
Epidemic threshold (SIR)
$$T_c = \frac{\langle k\rangle}{\langle k^2\rangle - \langle k\rangle}$$

$T$ transmissibility per link; outbreak possible for $T > T_c = 1/(\kappa-1)$.

Explore →
Random vaccination
$$g_c = 1 - \frac{1}{T(\kappa - 1)}$$

$g_c$ coverage that stops an outbreak of transmissibility $T$.

Explore →
Resolution limit
$$\Delta Q = \frac{l_{AB}}{L} - \frac{k_A k_B}{2L^2}$$

Merging $A$, $B$ joined by $l_{AB} = 1$ link raises $Q$ when $k_A k_B < 2L$.

Glossary

Adjacency matrix
N×N matrix with A_ij = 1 when nodes i and j are linked.
AUC
Probability that a true link outscores a non-link; 0.5 = guessing.
Assortative
Hubs tend to link to hubs (r > 0, k_nn(k) increasing).
Betweenness
Share of shortest paths between other pairs that pass through a node.
Bipartite network
Two node types, links only between types; its projection links nodes sharing a neighbour.
CCDF
Complementary cumulative distribution P(K ≥ k); needs no binning.
Clustering coefficient
Share of a node's neighbour pairs that are linked to each other.
Component
Maximal set of nodes connected by paths; the largest is the giant component.
Configuration model
Random network with a prescribed degree sequence, built by pairing stubs.
Degree
Number of links of a node.
Degree-preserving randomization
Repeated link swaps that keep every degree; a null model for everything but the degrees.
Diameter
Largest shortest-path distance in a network.
Disassortative
Hubs tend to link to small-degree nodes (r < 0).
Fiedler vector
Laplacian eigenvector of the second-smallest eigenvalue; its sign gives a two-way cut.
Fitness
Intrinsic attractiveness η of a node in the Bianconi–Barabási model.
Friendship paradox
A random neighbour has on average ⟨k²⟩/⟨k⟩ links, more than a random node.
G(N, L), G(N, p)
Random networks with exactly L links (Erdős–Rényi) or with each pair linked with probability p (Gilbert).
Hub
A node with far more links than average.
κ (kappa)
⟨k²⟩/⟨k⟩, mean degree of a node reached along a link.
k_min
Smallest degree from which a power-law tail is fitted.
KS distance
Largest gap between the data's cumulative distribution and the model's.
Log-binning
Bins growing by a constant factor, so that each bin collects enough nodes.
Modularity
Excess of links inside groups compared with the configuration model.
Node embedding
A vector per node, close for nodes close in the network.
Natural cutoff
Expected largest degree of a power law in a network of N nodes, k_min N^{1/(γ−1)}.
Null model
A random reference network that keeps some properties of the data and randomises the rest.
Percolation
Appearance or loss of a giant component as nodes or links are kept with some probability.
Path / walk
A walk is any sequence of linked nodes; a path visits each node at most once.
Preferential attachment
New links favour well-connected nodes, Π(k) ∝ k.
Resolution limit
Modularity merges communities with total degree below about √(2L).
Scale-free network
Network whose degree distribution follows a power law, p_k ~ k^−γ.
Small world
Average distance growing like ln N or slower.
Sparse network
L ≪ L_max, that is ⟨k⟩ ≪ N.
Transmissibility
Probability T that an infection passes along one link.
Ultra-small world
Distances growing like ln ln N, as in scale-free networks with 2 < γ < 3.
